# Night of 13.09.2026 - what is predictable, what is not, and the standard that settled it

Continues reports/NIGHT_2026_09_12.md (the bank filter was withdrawn there as a look-ahead).

## The standard

A single train/exam split kept contradicting itself: the training window said "leave it alone",
the exam said "change it". So the bar was raised - a change is accepted only if it wins in at
least **four years out of five**, measured year by year, and anything chosen by a search must be
re-chosen **blind** (using early years only) before it is believed.

## The bank is real. The strongest evidence of the night

Mirror test - the same signals traded the opposite way:

| variant | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 | per trade |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the bank | 0.70 | 0.46 | 0.40 | 0.28 | 0.49 | **+0.0670R** |
| everything reversed | -0.10 | -0.08 | -0.08 | -0.07 | -0.12 | **-0.1186R** |
| reversed with take/stop swapped | -0.12 | -0.08 | -0.08 | -0.07 | -0.11 | -0.4371R (13.6% win) |
| random side, same times | -0.04 | -0.08 | +0.06 | -0.03 | -0.12 | -0.0299R |

The arithmetic closes exactly: a random side loses 0.0299R, which is the cost of trading, so the
harness hands out no free money. The bank sits 0.093R above that floor and its mirror sits the same
distance below it, losing in every single year. This is what a genuine asymmetry looks like.

Bootstrap on the same book (2000 resamples): average trade +0.0670R with a 5-95% range of
+0.055..+0.080R, never negative; monthly +3.59R with a range of +2.40..+4.91R, never negative. A
null model that keeps the win rate but randomises the sign reaches the bank's ratio in **0.00%** of
2000 draws. At 1.5% risk per trade that is **+5.4% a month (range +3.6..+7.4%) with the worst
drawdown in five years at -7.1% of the account**.

## Direction is not predictable. Five independent confirmations

1. the scenario model (11.09) - withdrawn, look-ahead in rsi4h
2. the 140-feature bank filter (12.09) - withdrawn, features joined one hour ahead
3. **374,000 touches of swing levels**: the bounce beats the break in exactly **50.0%** of cases,
   and stays between 48% and 51% in every context bucket (arrival speed, trend, volatility,
   volume, how many times the level was touched)
4. a model predicting the signed 24h move: **+0.35 split by coin alone, +0.03 split by coin AND
   time**. The first number was cross-sectional leakage - coins move together, so the model
   recognised the date from a neighbour
5. **order book depth**, 448 days across 8 coins, 10,755 hours: the imbalance of resting orders at
   1%, 2% and 5% from the mid separates nothing at 1h, 4h or 24h, and its sign flips between years

## Size IS predictable, but it does not convert into money

A model trained on half the coins before 2025 ranks the next 24 hours' range on the other coins
after 2025 at **+0.42**, with or without BTC columns, +0.40 in 2025 and +0.43 in 2026. It also
ranks the bank's own trades: quietest fifth **+0.093R**, noisiest fifth **+0.034R** on the exam.

Three ways to spend that knowledge, all rejected: skipping noisy signals (the training window's
best cut is "no cut"), sizing by expected range (0.32 -> 0.31), stretching take and stop with it
(year by year k=0 wins twice, k=1 twice, k=0.75 once).

## The engine lab: 840 complete strategies

12 triggers x 7 entry styles x 5 exits x 2 sides, each on every coin and every year.

**The first run produced 307 "survivors" with 93% win rates and ratios up to 3.9 - and it was a
bug.** Two lines sat next to each other: the same trigger, the same limit, profitable both LONG
(3.93) and SHORT (3.60). Both sides winning is impossible, so the harness was paying. Cause: with a
limit entry the take was credited inside the bar that filled the order, and that bar's extreme
often predates the fill. The bank's own harness has guarded against this since day one
(`ht[0] = False`); the new lab did not inherit the guard.

After the fix: **87 of 840 pass, the best ratio is 0.14** - less than half the bank's. Every
survivor is a SHORT, which matches a second, independent measurement: after a 5 ATR rise the
give-back exceeds the continuation in 51.0% of cases, after a 5 ATR fall the bounce exceeds it in
only 47.9%. Rises fade, falls continue.

## Combining engines: the difference between chosen-after and chosen-blind

Picking the two least-correlated lab engines **by their full-history performance** and giving them
15% of the risk looked like the night's breakthrough: ratio 0.31 -> 0.38, profit +3.5 -> +4.0R per
month, drawdown -11.1 -> -10.3R.

Re-chosen **blind** on 2022-2024 only, the same procedure picks a short engine (correlation -0.26
with the bank) and the exam says: 15% -> ratio 0.25, 25% -> 0.17, 50% -> 0.11, against 0.30 for the
bank alone. The gain was the search looking at the answer. Withdrawn.

## Everything else that was tried and failed

| dead-volume drift (-0.10 ATR over 4h, same sign in all five years) | relative, not absolute; neither a short nor a neutral pair collects it |

| alternative exits: partial, trail 0.75-2.5 ATR, 24h/72h holds | trail wins 4/5 on average trade but only 3/5 on ratio - the bank's edge IS the quick 0.75 ATR pop |

## Where this leaves the account

The bank, unchanged and still switched off: 2.0 trades/day, 85.7% win rate, +0.067R per trade,
+3.59R/month, worst drawdown -4.8R over 52 months.

| risk per trade | per month | worst drawdown seen |
|---|---|---|
| 1.5% | +5.4% | -7.1% |
| 3% | +10.8% | -14.2% |
| 5% | +18.0% | -23.7% |

The arithmetic the owner should see plainly: a 90% win rate and a large profit cannot coexist. A
90% hit rate requires the take to sit near a quarter of the stop, which caps the edge around
+0.15R per trade. Large profit lives in rare big moves at 35-40% hit rates - the daily trend engine
measured tonight does exactly that, with a drawdown three times worse per unit of profit. The bank
sits at the high-win-rate end by design, and nothing tested in two nights moves it further.

## The stops, opened one by one (the owner asked for this directly)

337 stops out of 2790 trades (12.1%), average loss -1.018R.

* **They never work.** Before turning, a losing trade's best moment is +0.10R (median). **Not one
  stop in five years ever reached even half the take (0.375R).** So there is no "gave the profit
  back" problem to fix - the losers simply never go anywhere.
* **They are slow.** A winner reaches its take in 2.5 hours (median); a loser drags 11 hours.
* **They arrive in packs**: 43% of stops land within six hours of another one.
* **Nothing at entry sees them coming.** Twenty measurements of the tape at the moment of entry -
  moves over 4/12/24/72h, RSI 14 and 48, distance to SMA50 and SMA200, volatility regime, ATR as a
  share of price, volume against its day, candle range and body, position in the 72h range, the
  hour, BTC's own moves - and the difference between stops and takes **flips sign between years in
  every single one**. The mistake is not visible when it is made.
* By rule: #0 btc_pump_evening stops 6.8% of the time, #1 coin_run_evening 15.2%. By coin: SOL 7%,
  LINK 9%, ADA 10% against SUI 15%, TAO 15%, ZEC 16%.

### What was then tried against them, and what it cost

| idea | stops | profit | ratio | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| exit at market after 2h without a take | 13.0% -> **2.8%** | +3.6 -> +1.4R/mo | 0.32 -> 0.08 | no |
| after 6h, only if under water | 13.0% -> 7.9% | +3.6 -> +2.8R/mo | 0.32 -> 0.25 | no |
| cap of 6 positions per side | - | +3.6 -> +3.2R/mo | 0.32 -> 0.32 | no (3/5 years) |
| cap of 1 position per side | - | +3.6 -> +1.4R/mo | 0.32 -> 0.23 | no, but 42/52 positive months and worst month -3.4R instead of -4.6R |
| risk weighted by coin, learned on 2022-24 | - | +3.5 -> +3.8R/mo | 0.31 -> 0.34 | looked like 5/5, but re-learned every year walk-forward it wins **1 of 4** |
| risk weighted by rule | - | +3.5 -> +3.9R/mo | 0.31 -> 0.29 | no |
| regime switch: bank when BTC is up, shorts when down | - | +3.6 -> +8.8R/mo | 0.32 -> 0.15 | no |

Stops can be cut to a quarter of their number in an afternoon. Every way of doing it costs more
than the stops do, because the trades that hang around for hours contain the slow winners too.

## The pattern of the whole night

Six separate "improvements" looked convincing and then evaporated under an honest re-selection:
the 140-feature filter, the adaptive cut, the lab pair, the trail, the coin weights, the engine
mixes. In every case the difference was the same - the choice had been made while looking at the
years used to praise it. The only things that survived are the ones nobody chose: the bank itself,
and the fact that its mirror loses in all five years.

## Every component of the bank, checked one at a time

| component | alternatives tried | result |
|---|---|---|
| regime gate (4h trend engine holds the coin) | coin above SMA50 / SMA200, BTC above its 50-day, price above yesterday's, no gate | current gate 0.32; the best alternative 0.19, none wins more than 1 year of 5 |
| entry level (the signal bar's close) | 0.1 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 1.0 ATR deeper, waiting 15m to 4h | current 0.32; best alternative 0.28 (3/5). Deeper entries improve the average trade slightly and lose more in missed trades |

| take and stop multiples | 20 pairs from 0.5/2.0 to 1.5/4.0 | chosen blind on 2022-24 the winner is 1.0/2.5, which scores 0.25 on the exam against the current 0.31. Every top-5 early pair uses take 1.0, but which one wins does not transfer |
| coin list | 31 coins, and 12 chosen blind on early years | the pinned 16 stay best (0.32 vs 0.20 and 0.27); no unpinned coin earns a place |
| clock | +15, +30, +45 minute grids | the edge survives but halves - part of the thresholds is fitted to the hourly grid |

## What the fill style is worth (this one is practical)

| how the order gets in | per trade | per month | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| touch, market order, taker + 3bps slippage (live today) | +0.0612R | +3.3R | 0.29 |
| **resting limit filled when price trades through** | **+0.0685R** | **+3.7R** | **0.33** |
| resting limit needing 0.05% beyond the level | +0.0591R | +2.9R | 0.21 |
| resting limit needing 0.1% beyond | +0.0470R | +2.1R | 0.13 |
| touch with slippage doubled to 0.06% | +0.0486R | +2.6R | 0.19 |

A resting limit is worth about 12% more profit as long as small orders fill on the first pass -
which is the normal case for a $120 account - and turns into a loss if the queue makes price travel
0.05% past the level first. Separately: **letting slippage double costs a third of the profit**,
which is more than anything else measured in two nights.

## Re-mining the rulebook: the clearest demonstration of the night

The bank's nine rules were found once, years ago. They were mined again from scratch with three
guards that no earlier search had: a rule must pay in 2022, 2023 and 2024 *separately*, its mirror
must lose, and it must fire at least 80 times. 72 conditions, 2556 pairs, both sides - **275 pairs
passed everything**. The best five, chosen for coverage rather than raw score, were assembled into
a book the size of the current bank.

| | training 2022-24 | exam 2025-26 |
|---|---|---|
| the live bank | +0.0756R a trade, ratio 0.44 | 2.0 trades/day, 85% win, +0.0562R, **ratio 0.31** |
| freshly mined book | **+0.1354R a trade, ratio 0.51** | 0.5 trades/day, 81% win, +0.0306R, **ratio 0.04** |

Twice as good where it was mined, and nothing where it was not. 2025 came out at exactly zero.

That is the answer to "search a million scenarios": searching a million scenarios over five years
of data produces hundreds of beautiful curves by construction. The current rules are trustworthy
precisely because they were mined under even harder constraints - stability in every half-year and
confirmation on 22 coins that were not used to find them - and then survived years of live use.

Seven candidate improvements were found and withdrawn in one night: the 140-feature filter, the
rolling cut, the lab pair, the trailing exit, the coin weights, the take/stop pair, and now the
re-mined rulebook. Every one of them was chosen while looking at the years used to praise it.

## No survivorship bias in the edge

The pinned coins are all alive and liquid today, so the obvious worry is that the edge is a hidden
bet on coins that happened to survive. It is not. Across every coin in the data:

    correlation between "how much the coin rose" and "how much the bank made on it":  -0.00
    coins that FELL over the window (20 of them):  +0.0587R a trade, 85% win rate
    coins that ROSE (10):                          +0.0461R a trade, 84% win rate

OP fell 96% and the bank made +0.0908R a trade on it; DOT fell 94% (+0.0545R); ADA fell 75%
(+0.1030R). PEPE rose 257% and returned only +0.0558R. Three coins of thirty have a negative edge
(BICO, BNB, FIL). Pinned coins average +0.0664R against +0.0426R for the rest, which is why the
pinned list stays.

Direction barely matters. What defines a losing month is that the stops arrive in packs - three

The quiet-market observation does not convert into a gate: by volatility band the *quietest*
regime has the best average trade (+0.0862R) and only the narrow 0.76-0.81 band is negative

thresholds wins at most 2 years of 5.

## Pyramiding, and the eighth candidate to die

Adding a second position in the same coin - but only while the first one is already in profit - is
a classic and it looked like the night's best find: exam ratio 0.67 against 0.41, profit +45%, and
drawdown slightly lower. The mirror check passed too: adding to a LOSING position scored worse.

Then the look-ahead turned up in my own code. The "is it in profit" test read the close of the 15m
bar in which the entry happens - a price that is only known fifteen minutes after the decision.

With the last CLOSED bar used instead:

| variant | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 | beats base |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| one position (as now) | 0.72 | 0.52 | 0.42 | 0.34 | 0.57 | ratio 0.41 |
| up to 2 while the first is in profit | 0.61 | 0.48 | 0.36 | 0.42 | 0.63 | 2 of 5, ratio 0.41 |
| up to 3 | 0.64 | 0.49 | 0.40 | 0.41 | 0.52 | 1 of 5 |
| up to 2 while the first is **losing** | 0.56 | 0.48 | 0.59 | 0.35 | 0.52 | 2 of 5, ratio 0.43 |

Adding to losers now scores as well as adding to winners, which says the profit condition carries
no information at all. It is the same result as everywhere else tonight: whether a move continues
is not knowable - not even when the trade is already winning.

## The clock, and the live-code parity check

Hours of the day: no three-hour block is negative in every year, so entry time by itself carries
nothing. Weekdays: **Monday is the only negative day** (-0.0140R a trade, 79.1% win rate against
83-89% elsewhere), and it was negative in 2022, 2025 and 2026 but positive in 2023 and 2024.
Dropping it would give ratio 0.39 against 0.32 and a -9.5R drawdown against -11.1R - but three
years of five is not enough, and the two negative years being the most recent is exactly how
recency fools a search. Left in, with a note to re-check after 2027.

**Parity with the live module.** Every number in this report comes from the research harness; the
money would be made by src/pullback_live.py. Driven hour by hour over the last 60 days of history,
with a candle store that only ever returns closed bars, the live module produced:

    all 40 of the research trades - nothing missed
    entry levels identical to the last digit: 55 of 55
    15 extra watches, all of them signals the research skips because the coin is already busy -
    the live module applies that constraint later, at entry, against the exchange position

So the engine that would trade is the engine that was measured.

## The one that almost made it: a target that moves with the expected range

The only thing tonight's machine can predict is how far price will travel. Scaling the TAKE with it
while leaving the stop exactly where it is (so the money risked per trade never changes) is the
cleanest possible use of that:

    take = 0.75 ATR x (expected 24h range / its usual value) ^ 0.5      stop stays at 3 ATR

Chosen blind on 2022-24, it wins four years of five on the ratio, ties the fifth, and improves the
average trade in **all five**. The mirror behaves correctly: scaling the take the other way (wider
target when a quiet market is expected) scores 0.27 and 0.30 against the base 0.32, so the effect
is the logic and not the harness.

Then the range model was refitted every year on prior data only, as a live bot would have it:

| year | as now | take scaled by expected range |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | +0.0713R, ratio 0.46 | **+0.0779R**, ratio 0.41 |
| 2024 | +0.0680R, ratio 0.40 | **+0.0699R**, ratio 0.32 |
| 2025 | +0.0536R, ratio 0.28 | **+0.0584R**, ratio **0.32** |
| 2026 | +0.0638R, ratio 0.52 | **+0.0683R**, ratio 0.50 |

**The average trade improves in four years out of four** (+7% to +9%), and the ratio improves in
one. The drawdown grows faster than the profit does. Under the rule this report has used
throughout, that is a rejection - but it is the only candidate of two nights whose gain is
consistent in every year tested, and it is the honest option if the owner ever decides he wants
more profit per trade and will pay for it in drawdown.

## Does the edge fade, and do bad months cluster?

Quarter by quarter across eighteen quarters, the average trade has a slope of **-0.0023R per
quarter against an error of +-0.0019** - a t of -1.23, indistinguishable from zero. The first half
of the history averages +0.0735R and the second +0.0559R, a mild tilt well inside the noise. One
quarter of eighteen was negative (2024Q3, -0.0138R).

More important for an owner who fears losing months:

| | next month on average | positive |
|---|---|---|
| after a LOSING month | **+5.17R** | **10 of 12** |
| after a winning month | +3.12R | 29 of 39 |

A bad month is followed by a better-than-usual one, and **the longest run of consecutive losing
months in five years is two**. The drawdown does not stretch into a quarter; it is worked off in a
month or two.

## The gate's own internals - the last knob in the system

A coin is tradable only while the 4h trend engine holds it, and that engine's settings were chosen
for itself, never for the bank. Moved one at a time:

| change | ratio | beats base |
|---|---|---|
| **as now** (breakout 20, EMA 50, stop 2 ATR, trail 3 ATR, BTC calm gate 7.5%) | **0.32** | - |
| stop 1.5 ATR | 0.34 | 3 of 5 |
| breakout 30 | 0.32 | 2 of 5 |
| EMA 100 | 0.26 | 3 of 5 |
| trail 2 ATR | 0.23 | 1 of 5 |
| BTC gate 15% | 0.21 | 1 of 5 |
| **BTC calm gate off** | **0.20** | **0 of 5** |

Nothing passes. And the inherited BTC calm gate turns out to be worth a third of the result
(0.32 against 0.20 without it) - a setting nobody picked for this engine holds it up.

## The audit is complete

Tested and rejected across two nights, each under the four-of-five-years rule with blind
re-selection where a search was involved: the 140-feature filter, the rolling cut, the lab pair of
engines, trailing exits, coin weights, rule weights, take/stop multiples, the ATR period, a
re-mined rulebook, pyramiding, a take scaled by predicted range, break-even stops, early time
exits, position caps, volatility sizing, streak guards, regime switching, wider coin lists, deeper
entries, phase-shifted clocks, weekday and hour filters, funding as carry and as a filter, and the
gate's own internals.

Eleven of those looked like improvements at first pass. None survived. Two of them were look-aheads
inside my own code, found before any of them reached the owner.

What remains are the two levers that are not strategy at all: **how much risk goes on each trade**,
and **how well the orders are executed**. The second is worth more than every idea in this report -
a resting limit instead of a market touch is +12%, and letting slippage double is -33%.

## The candles themselves

Everything above rests on 15m bars, and a single bad print - a wick that never traded - can hand
the backtest a take it never earned, especially with a target only 0.75 ATR away. Suspicious bars
were flagged (a wick over 8 ATR with almost no body, zero volume on a bar that moved, a range ten
times its neighbours', a gap over 5 ATR that closes immediately):

    1,698 suspicious bars out of 1,967,076   (0.086%)
    trades that EXIT on one:   44 of 2790  (1.58%) - 33 takes, 11 stops
    trades that ENTER on one:  11 of 2790  (0.39%)

Dropping every trade touched by one of them leaves 2741 trades and **improves** the result: average
trade +0.0697R against +0.0670R, ratio 0.34 against 0.32. Bad prints were costing the strategy
rather than flattering it, which is the answer one wants: the backtest is not built on phantom
wicks.
